Concepts

zbrain treats a small set of concepts as the trust boundary: OKF-style claim documents, immutable evidence snapshots, and a disposable index that is rebuilt from canonical Markdown. Everything below traces to the shipped trust model.

What a claim is

Trusted claims are OKF-style Markdown documents with a typed frontmatter block. The shipped profile is zbrain.trusted-memory/v1:

type: zbrain.claim
zbrain:
  profile: zbrain.trusted-memory/v1
  id: clm_<32 lowercase hex characters>
  tier: axioms | mental-models | projects | decisions
  basis: owner | evidence | derived
status: draft | approved | superseded | revoked

zbrain.id is the stable trust identity; the path is not the identity. Approved claims carry a verification digest over their canonical rendered content.

Basis

Every claim carries a basis, recorded in the frontmatter and passed with --basis <owner|evidence|derived> on claim draft:

  • owner

    Claims asserted directly by the owner. The getting-started tour drafts and approves one with --basis owner and no further references.

  • evidence

    Evidence-based claims must pass an evidence ID from evidence add using --evidence.

  • derived

    Derived claims must reference approved supporting claims or verified evidence.

Tiers

Claim concepts live in four tiers, recorded in the frontmatter and passed with --tier <tier> on claim draft and claim supersede:

  • axioms
  • mental-models
  • projects
  • decisions

claim supersede accepts a --tier for the replacement claim.

Lifecycle

Every claim follows an explicit lifecycle: draft -> approved -> superseded|revoked.

  • claim draft writes an OKF claim candidate. The claim body is read from stdin; drafts are never trusted answer material.
  • claim approve validates the claim basis and referenced evidence or supporting claims, then records verified.at, verified.by, and verified.digest.
  • Approved claims are replaced through supersession, not in-place editing. Only valid approved claims enter trusted results.
  • claim supersede <id> is how approved claims are replaced: the replacement carries the supplied --tier, --title, and --basis. Supersession, not in-place editing.
  • claim revoke <id> --reason <text> marks a claim revoked with a recorded reason.

Use migrate okf for legacy schema: zbrain.claim/v1 files. It reports whether reapproval is required; migration does not silently make an old approval trusted.

Evidence

evidence add copies an already-local file into an immutable evidence snapshot and records its metadata: origin, capture time, media type, byte length, and SHA-256.

  • Evidence snapshots are immutable local copies; raw evidence is not indexed or treated as trusted instructions.

Claims cite snapshots by evidence ID (--evidence <id>); claim approve validates the claim basis and the referenced evidence or supporting claims before recording the verification digest.

Trust and retrieval outcomes

Retrieval is fail-closed with visible ready, gap, and blocked outcomes. ask checks index freshness and the returned claim's canonical binding and trust dependencies before returning JSON.

  • ready

    A matching valid approved claim passes validation and is returned as trusted context JSON.

  • gap

    No matching approved claim produces status: "gap". A gap is a trusted-memory miss, not permission to use drafts or raw evidence. Add or approve a valid claim, then reindex.

  • blocked

    Explicit claim conflicts return status: "blocked". The matched approved claims contain an explicit conflict; resolve it through the claim lifecycle.

Promotion candidates

Drafts are returned, when matched, only as promotion_candidates. They are never trusted answer material; promotion is a separate step in the lifecycle.

Fail-closed rules

  • reindex

    reindex rejects invalid claims, invalid evidence, broken supporting-claim closures, and stale or legacy-unindexed trust inputs without rewriting them.

  • ask

    ask fails closed for dirty, missing, stale, or rejected indexes.

See the CLI reference for the commands behind these rules, and Getting started for the workflow in practice.

Trust model at a glance

The shipped trust model, in full:

  • zbrain.id is the stable trust identity; the path is not the identity.
  • Approved claims are replaced through supersession, not in-place editing.
  • Only valid approved claims enter trusted results.
  • Drafts are returned, when matched, only as promotion_candidates.
  • Approved claims carry a verification digest over their canonical rendered content.
  • Evidence snapshots are immutable local copies; raw evidence is not indexed or treated as trusted instructions.
  • reindex rejects invalid claims, invalid evidence, broken supporting-claim closures, and stale or legacy-unindexed trust inputs without rewriting them.
  • ask fails closed for dirty, missing, stale, or rejected indexes.
  • Explicit claim conflicts return status: "blocked"; no matching trusted claim returns status: "gap".
